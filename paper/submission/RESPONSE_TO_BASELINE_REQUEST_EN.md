# Prepared response to a possible domain-baseline request

This is an unsent response draft, not a claim that a reviewer has raised the issue or that additional experiments were performed.

**Possible comment:** Please compare with DeepHawkes and CasFlow.

**Proposed response:** We agree that DeepHawkes and CasFlow are relevant domain methods and discuss them in the related work. A valid numerical comparison would require re-evaluating them on the same eligible roots and train/validation/test roles, at the same observation windows, with the same root-relative response endpoint, event-counting rules, available input information and evaluation functional. Their published logarithmic point-error values cannot be inserted into our tables as comparable measurements: our primary endpoint is discrete CRPS for a predictive count distribution, whereas Supplementary Section S8.3 reports MSLE of the already saved predictive medians. A predictive median is not generally the point forecast that minimizes squared logarithmic error. A point output alone also does not specify a count distribution for CRPS; conversely, a model that represents uncertainty must still supply the forecast law under this aligned contract. Our primary estimand is the paired change from I0 to I1 within each fixed forecasting family, under shared validation-selected settings. It therefore does not depend on an unaligned between-method ranking. We make no claim to outperform DeepHawkes, CasFlow or other specialized cascade architectures, and explicitly identify the absence of an aligned domain-method comparison as a limitation. Such a comparison would extend the scope of the study rather than validate a superiority claim made here.

**Where this is supported:** Introduction and Related Work; Methods/Evaluation; Limitations; Supplement S2.3 and S8.3. This response acknowledges the missing comparison and does not guarantee that an editor will accept the narrower scope. Do not say that new experiments were added.
